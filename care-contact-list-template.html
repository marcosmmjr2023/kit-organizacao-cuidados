Care contact list

When a care question appears, a phone number alone rarely says whom to contact, when they are available or what they agreed to handle. Use these two pages with the person receiving care to record a small, current network of confirmed contacts, preferred routes and backups.

This is a coordination list, not an emergency plan or clinical directory. Confirm every role and contact detail with the person or service concerned. Use the local emergency number for urgent emergencies and follow current professional instructions. Do not infer availability, authority or consent from someone appearing on this sheet.

Build a useful list without collecting too much

  1. Start with purpose. Include only contacts needed for a specific care coordination task.
  2. Ask permission. Confirm that the person receiving care agrees with who is listed and what may be shared.
  3. Verify directly. Check the spelling, role, preferred contact route and availability with each person or service.
  4. Name a backup. Record an alternative route for important tasks; do not assume someone is always available.
  5. Date every review. Remove outdated copies and recheck the list after any change in the care network.

Privacy and safe sharing

A completed contact list can expose private relationships, health services and routines. Record the minimum needed, store it securely and share it only with the person’s agreement and people involved in the relevant task. Do not write passwords, access codes, financial details, full identification numbers or clinical history. If a paper copy is lost or outdated, replace it and dispose of the old copy securely.
