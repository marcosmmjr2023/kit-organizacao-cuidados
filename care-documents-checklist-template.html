Care documents checklist

Care paperwork becomes hard to use when old copies, missing review dates and unrelated documents travel together. Use these two pages to identify the current version, record where it is securely stored and prepare only what a specific appointment actually requires.

This checklist records document status and location; it is not a substitute for any original document or professional instruction. Confirm requirements with the relevant service. Record a safe location or short reference—not full identification, insurance, account or medical-record numbers. Do not carry original documents unless the service specifically requires them.

Create a current, minimal document map

  1. Ask what is needed. Confirm requirements with the appointment, service or agency instead of taking the whole file.
  2. Identify the current copy. Record its title, issue or review date and a safe location reference.
  3. Assign review. Give every expiry, renewal or missing item an owner and next-check date.
  4. Protect originals. Decide whether a copy is accepted and how papers will return to secure storage.
  5. Retire superseded copies. Mark them clearly and dispose of them securely when retention is no longer required.

Privacy, consent and access

A document map can reveal sensitive relationships and services even without showing the documents themselves. Involve the person receiving care, confirm who may access each item and share only what the recipient needs for the stated purpose. Do not write passwords, access codes, full identity or insurance numbers, financial details or unnecessary clinical history on this checklist. Keep completed sheets securely and review access whenever roles change.
