Care expense tracker

Care costs can become hard to reconcile when several relatives buy supplies, pay for transport or wait for reimbursement. Use these two pages to record only verified transactions, identify who paid and make every unsettled item visible without storing sensitive banking details.

This is an administrative record, not financial, legal or benefits advice. Copy amounts and dates from receipts or statements, keep estimates clearly marked and confirm reimbursement rules with the relevant person, insurer or service. Do not use this sheet as proof that a debt, reimbursement or eligibility decision has been accepted.

Keep a care expense record that can be checked

  1. Agree on scope. Decide with the person receiving care which expenses belong in this shared record.
  2. Record facts, not guesses. Use the transaction date and verified amount; label any estimate until it is confirmed.
  3. Reference receipts safely. Write a short reference or storage location instead of copying full card, account or claim numbers.
  4. Separate payment from reimbursement. “Paid” does not mean “reimbursed”; record both statuses and the responsible follow-up.
  5. Reconcile regularly. Review totals and unresolved items with the people involved before archiving the period.

Privacy and shared money records

Expense records can expose health needs, routines and financial relationships. Record only what is needed for the agreed purpose and share it only with the person’s permission and the people handling that expense. Do not write passwords, PINs, security codes, full card or bank account numbers, login details or full benefit identifiers. Keep receipts separately in a secure location and remove outdated copies.
