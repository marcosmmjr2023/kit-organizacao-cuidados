Care medication inventory

Running out, keeping expired packs and storing duplicate supplies in different places are administrative problems a simple inventory can expose. Use these two pages to record only what you physically checked and to assign refill or disposal questions for follow-up.

An inventory is not a medication list or dosing schedule. Copy names and directions only from the current pharmacy label or professional medication list. Do not use this sheet to start, stop, replace, split, combine or change the timing or dose of any medication. Ask a pharmacist or the appropriate professional about safe storage, expiry, disposal, missed doses and discrepancies.

Count supplies without making clinical decisions

  1. Choose one review date. Count only containers you can physically check.
  2. Keep the source visible. Transcribe the exact name and strength from the current label; do not rely on memory.
  3. Record facts separately. Quantity, expiry and storage location are observations—not instructions to use the item.
  4. Flag uncertainty. Mark discrepancies, damaged labels, duplicates and disposal questions for a pharmacist or appropriate professional.
  5. Assign follow-up. Give each refill or question an owner, contact route, date and status.

Privacy, storage and disposal

Medication labels and completed inventories can reveal sensitive health information. Record only what is necessary, store pages securely and share them only with the person’s agreement and those involved in the task. Do not include passwords, payment details or full identification numbers. Keep medicines in their labelled containers and follow pharmacist or manufacturer guidance for storage and disposal.
