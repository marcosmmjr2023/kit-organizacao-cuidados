Caregiver handover checklist

A good handoff separates facts from assumptions and confirmed plans from open questions. These two pages help family caregivers pass along the current situation, upcoming commitments and unfinished follow-ups without turning a household note into a clinical record.

Use this for organization, not medical decisions. Record only information you know is current and necessary. Do not use this sheet to diagnose symptoms, change medication, set doses or replace instructions from a qualified professional. Contact the appropriate professional or emergency service when needed.

A five-minute handover

  1. Write the period covered. Identify who is handing over and who has confirmed the next shift.
  2. Describe observable facts. Note what happened and when, without guessing a cause or diagnosis.
  3. Separate done, due and unconfirmed. Never make an unchecked task look complete.
  4. Name the next action. Give each open follow-up a responsible person and deadline only after confirmation.
  5. Review aloud. The incoming caregiver repeats urgent logistics and asks about unclear items.

Final check

  • The next caregiver confirmed the handoff
  • Open items have not been marked complete
  • Upcoming times and transport were checked
  • Only necessary information was shared
  • Questions are directed to the right professional
  • No passwords or full document numbers are written here

Privacy and record hygiene

Keep the completed pages where only the care team can access them. Avoid passwords, banking details, full identification numbers and unrelated clinical details. Dispose of outdated copies safely. If medication information must be passed along, copy only current instructions supplied by a qualified professional and clearly identify the source and date.
