Caregiver task checklist

A useful checklist makes agreed work visible without assigning it by assumption. These two pages map recurring tasks from the current care routine, record who accepted each one and separate completion from anything that still needs follow-up.

Copy confirmed tasks; do not create care instructions. Add only work already agreed with the person receiving care and, where relevant, documented in a current professional care plan. A person becomes the owner only after they accept the task.

How to build a caregiver task checklist

  1. Start from current sources. Use the agreed household routine, current care plan and up-to-date appointment information.
  2. Write a verifiable action. Replace vague labels such as “help” with a concrete administrative task and a clear time or trigger.
  3. Confirm capability and authority. Do not assign clinical, financial or legal actions to someone who is not prepared or authorized.
  4. Ask the owner. Record a name only after the person explicitly accepts the task and its timing.
  5. Keep open work visible. A blank owner or completion box is a gap to resolve, not evidence that someone else will do it.

Safety and privacy boundaries

Do not use this checklist to diagnose symptoms, decide treatment, change medication, invent procedures or delay urgent help. “Done” records an action, not a clinical result. Follow the current care plan and contact the appropriate professional or local emergency service when needed. Do not write passwords, door codes, full identity numbers, payment details or unnecessary health information.
