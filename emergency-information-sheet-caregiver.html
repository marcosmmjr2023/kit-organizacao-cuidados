Emergency information sheet for caregivers

In a stressful moment, a short sheet can help a family caregiver find verified contacts and point responders to current records. Prepare it calmly with the person receiving care, then review it regularly.

Call emergency services first when urgent help is needed. This sheet is a locator, not an emergency plan or clinical record. Copy only information confirmed by the person, their current professional records or an authorized decision-maker. Never use it to diagnose symptoms, choose treatment or delay a call for help.

How to prepare the sheet

  1. Include the person. Ask what name, communication support and contacts they want available, and who may see the completed page.
  2. Verify every entry. Check phone numbers and record locations against a current source; do not rely on memory.
  3. Point to clinical records. Write where the current medication list, allergy record or care plan is kept instead of copying details that can become stale.
  4. Keep access practical and safe. Choose an agreed location that caregivers can find without writing passwords, alarm codes or hidden-key details.
  5. Date and review it. Replace old copies after every confirmed change and at the next scheduled review.

Limits and privacy

This sheet does not prove consent, legal authority, diagnosis, allergies, medication use or treatment instructions. Emergency responders and current professional records remain the authoritative sources. Keep details minimal, store the completed pages securely and remove access when it is no longer needed. If information is uncertain, label it as unverified rather than guessing.
