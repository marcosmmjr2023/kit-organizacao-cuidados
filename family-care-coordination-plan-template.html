Family care coordination plan

A schedule shows when care happens; this plan shows how agreed priorities, roles, records and review points fit together. Complete it with the person receiving care and keep every unconfirmed responsibility visibly open.

Coordinate existing decisions; do not create clinical instructions. Copy health-related directions only from the current plan or document issued by the relevant professional. This sheet does not authorize treatment, prove consent or assign a task to someone who has not accepted it.

How to build the plan

  1. Start with the person's priorities. Record what they want help coordinating, what they prefer to do independently and who may receive updates.
  2. Use current sources. Point to the current schedule, professional care plan, medicine list or contact record instead of copying sensitive detail.
  3. Confirm every role. A name belongs in the owner column only after that person accepts the responsibility.
  4. Leave gaps visible. Mark missing coverage, unknown contacts and undecided follow-ups as open rather than guessing.
  5. Set a review trigger. Review on the agreed date and after a discharge, schedule change or change requested by the person.

What should remain unchanged until reviewed?

Review check

  • Person's priorities are still current
  • Every named owner has accepted
  • Open coverage gaps remain visible
  • Linked records use current versions
  • Sharing permissions were reconfirmed
  • Old printed copies will be removed

Limits and privacy

This is an administrative coordination aid, not a clinical care plan, medical or legal advice, proof of authority, or an emergency protocol. Do not use it to diagnose symptoms or start, stop or change treatment. Do not write passwords, door codes, full identity numbers, payment details or unnecessary diagnoses. Store completed pages securely and share only what is needed and agreed.
