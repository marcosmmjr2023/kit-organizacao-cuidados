Family care meeting agenda

Family care conversations can circle around the same concerns without producing a clear next step. Use these two pages to collect agenda items in advance, make room for the person receiving care and leave with only confirmed decisions, named owners and review dates.

This agenda supports coordination; it does not create consent, authority or a clinical care plan. Involve the person receiving care in decisions that affect them and document disagreement instead of presenting it as agreement. Confirm health, legal and financial questions with the appropriate professional.

Run a focused, person-centred meeting

  1. Ask before adding people. Confirm who the person receiving care wants involved and how they prefer to participate.
  2. Collect agenda items early. State each topic as a concrete question, concern or coordination gap.
  3. Separate facts from proposals. Mark what is confirmed, what still needs checking and what someone merely suggests.
  4. Do not force agreement. Record unresolved views and the safe next step for obtaining reliable information.
  5. Close every action. A task is not assigned until its owner confirms it and a check-in date is visible.

Share only what the group needs

Meeting notes may expose preferences, conflicts, availability and service relationships. Agree who receives the record, keep completed sheets securely and remove details that are not necessary for coordination. Do not write passwords, access codes, full account or document numbers, diagnoses or detailed medical history on this agenda. A person may change a preference or withdraw consent; record the change and review affected tasks.
