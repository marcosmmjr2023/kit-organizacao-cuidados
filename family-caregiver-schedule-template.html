Family caregiver schedule

A shared schedule works only when it distinguishes confirmed coverage from requests and identifies gaps before the week begins. These two pages help a family coordinate shifts, appointments, practical tasks and handoffs without silently assigning work to someone who has not agreed.

Confirm every assignment. A name on this template does not create consent, availability or competence. The person receiving care should participate whenever possible. Do not assign medication changes, clinical decisions or procedures to anyone who is not authorized and prepared. Follow the current care plan and contact the appropriate professional or emergency service when needed.

Build a schedule people can rely on

  1. List fixed commitments first. Add confirmed appointments, work constraints and existing care coverage.
  2. Describe the responsibility. Replace “help” with a specific task, time window and handoff point.
  3. Ask before assigning. Mark a person as confirmed only after they explicitly agree.
  4. Keep gaps visible. An open shift is safer than a name that implies coverage which does not exist.
  5. Review changes. Record who confirmed a swap and make sure the person receiving care knows the current plan.

Before sharing the schedule

  • Every covered period was explicitly confirmed
  • Open gaps remain clearly marked
  • Responsibilities are specific and realistic
  • Handoff times and contacts were checked
  • The person receiving care participated where possible
  • No passwords or unnecessary private details are included

Privacy and safe use

Share the completed schedule only with people who need it. Use the minimum necessary detail: times, responsibilities and preferred contact method rather than diagnoses, full identification numbers, passwords or financial information. Store it securely, remove outdated copies and review consent when the care team changes.
