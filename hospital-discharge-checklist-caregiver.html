Hospital discharge checklist

Leaving hospital can involve many instructions at once. Use these two pages with the person receiving care and the discharge team to record what has actually been confirmed, prepare the trip home and give every open follow-up a named owner.

This checklist organizes information; it does not provide medical advice or replace discharge instructions. Ask the discharge team to clarify anything uncertain. Copy medicine names, doses, timing, restrictions and warning signs only from the current written instructions. Do not start, stop, replace or change treatment using this sheet. For urgent concerns, follow the discharge team’s emergency directions or contact local emergency services.

How to prepare for a safer handover home

  1. Include the person. Ask what they want explained, who may receive information and who they want involved at home.
  2. Use teach-back. In your own words, repeat what will happen at home and ask the team to correct misunderstandings.
  3. Reconcile documents. Use the current discharge summary and medicine list; clearly mark older copies as superseded.
  4. Assign open tasks. A referral or equipment request is not complete until its status, owner and next check are recorded.
  5. Protect details. Carry only the information needed for the transition and store papers securely after arrival.

Privacy and version control

Discharge papers can contain sensitive health and identity information. Share them only with the person’s permission and people directly involved in the transition. Do not copy passwords, access codes, full insurance identifiers or unrelated history onto this checklist. Date each update, keep the current version easy to identify and securely destroy superseded copies when they are no longer needed.
